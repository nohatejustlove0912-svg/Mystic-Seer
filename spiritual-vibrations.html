The Great Misunderstanding About Spiritual Vibrations

By Jassy Dagasdas — Mystic Seer · 2026–2027 editorial collection

Spiritual discussions often describe joy and abundance as high vibration, while anger and grief are labeled low vibration. I question that simple hierarchy. Emotions have context: anger may arise in response to injustice, and happiness may accompany behavior that harms another person.

Material success is not proof of spiritual virtue. People can prosper through privilege, opportunity, skill or exploitation, and ethical people can experience hardship. We should not interpret someone's suffering as evidence that they failed to maintain a supposedly correct frequency.

In physics, frequency has a measurable meaning. In spiritual discourse, vibration is often used metaphorically to describe emotional or perceived energetic states. The metaphor does not establish a measurable moral frequency or a universal mechanism that rewards good behavior.

I prefer to evaluate intentions alongside actions, consequences and accountability. Grief does not make someone spiritually inferior, and positivity does not excuse harmful choices. A mature spiritual practice makes room for difficult emotions while encouraging ethical action and self-awareness.
