The Ethics and Responsibilities of a Psychic Practitioner

By Jassy Dagasdas — Mystic Seer · 2026–2027 editorial collection

Spiritual practitioners often meet people during uncertain or emotionally difficult periods. That creates a responsibility to communicate carefully, respect boundaries and avoid presenting interpretations as unquestionable truth. Professional confidence should never require pretending to know everything.

In my practice, informed consent means explaining the nature of the service, its limitations, fees and expectations before a session. Personal information and client stories should be handled with care. Public testimonials or case documentation should only be shared with appropriate permission and privacy protections.

I also believe practitioners should avoid fear-based claims that pressure clients into repeated sessions or expensive rituals. A reading cannot establish that someone has been cursed, diagnose illness or guarantee an outcome. When a concern falls outside spiritual practice, referral to an appropriate professional is the responsible response.

Accountability includes acknowledging uncertainty, correcting errors and respecting a client's right to disagree. My goal is to encourage discernment and personal agency. A meaningful spiritual relationship should not require dependence on the practitioner or the abandonment of independent judgment.
