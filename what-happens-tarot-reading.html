What Really Happens During a Tarot Reading?

By Jassy Dagasdas — Mystic Seer · 2026–2027 editorial collection

A tarot session begins with a question or area of concern. I use the imagery, relationships between cards and their positions in a spread to develop an interpretation. My intuitive impressions may inform that interpretation, but I do not present them as verified knowledge of hidden events.

Specific questions tend to support a more focused discussion. Instead of asking whether everything will be fine, a client might ask what patterns deserve attention in a current relationship or what considerations could help them evaluate a career change. The reading becomes a structured opportunity for reflection.

Cards do not establish medical diagnoses, prove infidelity or determine another person's consent. I encourage clients to evaluate real-world information and seek qualified advice where appropriate. A difficult card is not a guarantee of disaster, and a positive card is not a guarantee of success.

At the end of a session, I want clients to understand the themes discussed and identify choices they can consider independently. Tarot is not scientifically validated as a predictive method. In my practice, its purpose is symbolic exploration and spiritual guidance, not surrendering responsibility for one's life.
