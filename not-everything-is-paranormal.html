Not Everything Unexplained Is Paranormal

By Jassy Dagasdas — Mystic Seer · 2026–2027 editorial collection

A strange sound, an unusual photograph or an unsettling atmosphere can be the beginning of an investigation. It should not be the conclusion. My interest in paranormal narratives includes understanding why people interpret particular experiences as supernatural and what other explanations might account for them.

I begin with the account itself: what was observed, who was present, what conditions existed and whether the event can be reproduced. Lighting, reflections, animals, electrical equipment and building conditions are worth considering. Witnesses can be sincere while still misinterpreting an ambiguous event.

In fieldwork, tools such as EMF meters can document electromagnetic readings, but they do not identify spirits. Dowsing responses and psychic impressions are also not conclusive evidence of paranormal activity. I keep these observations separate from instrument readings and independently verifiable facts.

A case can remain unresolved without becoming proof of a haunting. If a property presents electrical, structural or environmental concerns, qualified specialists should assess them. My goal is to investigate with curiosity while respecting the people involved and avoiding unnecessary fear.
