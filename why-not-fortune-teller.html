Why I Don't Call Myself a Fortune Teller

By Jassy Dagasdas — Mystic Seer · 2026–2027 editorial collection

When someone asks me to tell their future, I first clarify what a reading means in my practice. I work with intuitive impressions, tarot symbolism and astrological interpretation. I do not claim to possess a complete or infallible account of what will happen next. The distinction matters because the language we use shapes what clients expect from us.

In my spiritual framework, impressions may be understood as messages or energetic information. That is a belief-based interpretation, not an independently verified communication. A responsible reader must be willing to say when a message is unclear, when a question exceeds their scope, or when there is simply not enough information to draw a conclusion.

Consider a client asking whether a partner will return. Rather than promising reconciliation, I would explore the relationship patterns represented in the reading, the client's boundaries and the choices available to them. A reading cannot establish another person's private intentions. Direct communication and observable behavior remain important.

I want clients to leave a session with more agency, not a new dependence on predictions. Use the reading as an opportunity to reflect, then make decisions with the information and support available in your actual life. Uncertainty is not a failure of spiritual practice; acknowledging it is part of professional responsibility.
