Can Spirituality and Science Coexist?

By Jassy Dagasdas — Mystic Seer · 2026–2027 editorial collection

I do not see spiritual reflection and critical inquiry as enemies. Spiritual traditions can offer personal meaning, symbolic language and practices for reflection. Science uses systematic observation and testing to investigate claims about the natural world. These are different functions, and neither should be misrepresented as the other.

A ritual may feel meaningful or calming to someone without proving that it changed an external event through supernatural means. Likewise, a scientific explanation for an unusual experience does not necessarily erase the personal meaning someone attaches to it. The distinction is between subjective significance and evidence for a causal claim.

In my own work, I separate what I intuitively interpret from what I can observe or verify. When new information contradicts an earlier interpretation, I believe it is important to acknowledge that rather than force the evidence to fit a preferred narrative.

Responsible spirituality leaves room for questions. It also respects the expertise of medical, psychological and other qualified professionals. We can explore beliefs without asking people to abandon evidence, and we can value personal experience without presenting it as universal proof.
