Intuition vs. Anxiety: Understanding the Difference

By Jassy Dagasdas — Mystic Seer · 2026–2027 editorial collection

An impression can feel urgent, convincing or deeply personal. That intensity alone does not tell us whether it reflects intuition, anxiety, memory or a reasonable response to something we have noticed. In my practice, I encourage people to examine an impression before assigning it spiritual meaning.

Start by describing the experience without interpreting it. Write down what happened, what you felt, what you already knew and what you fear might happen. Separate observable details from the story you are building around them. A journal can help reveal patterns, including occasions when an impression did not match subsequent events.

For example, a delayed reply from a partner may trigger a strong feeling that something is wrong. Before treating it as a psychic warning, consider the person's usual communication habits and other plausible explanations. A calm feeling is not proof of accuracy, just as a distressing feeling is not automatically false.

If worry repeatedly disrupts sleep, relationships or everyday functioning, seek support from a qualified mental health professional. Spiritual reflection can be personally meaningful, but it is not a diagnostic tool. Developing discernment means learning when to act on evidence, when to wait and when to ask for help.
